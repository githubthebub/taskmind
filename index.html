Why this app exists

Freedom has two halves. The outer half is money and skill — the means to run your own life. The inner half is a mind that stays yours: calm on demand, in love with the process, un-bossed by guilt or by gold. Svaraj trains both, a little every day, in four rooms — Sit (jhāna), Wheel (a whole life), Mind (the three muscles), and this daily loop — with a game of light running through it: every rep earns light, and the lamp grows from a spark toward sunrise. The fast path, honestly

Continuity beats heroics. Daily, same seat, same hour — pre-dawn if you can. Ten daily sits outrun one Sunday marathon.
Give it runway. Joy needs time to build. 30–45 minutes is where things start to happen.
Gladden the mind first. Sixty seconds of gratitude before anchoring. Jhāna grows out of gladness, not grit.
Hunt the pleasant. Any agreeable sensation — hands, chest, the breath itself — is a door. Attend to it and it widens.
Effort to arrive, letting go to enter. Striving carries you to the threshold and then becomes the obstacle. Release is the key that turns.
Stack a retreat day. One quiet Saturday — three or four sits with slow walks between — compresses weeks of dailies.

Most people reach steady access in weeks of honest dailies; first jhāna often lands on longer sits or a retreat day. If practice ever surfaces something heavy, ease off and find a teacher — speed is not worth capsizing. (Method here follows the Leigh Brasington school of “lite” jhāna — the most reachable on ordinary schedules.)

Mind

Money without a sovereign mind just changes who holds the leash. Three muscles, one rep a day: love the process, hold the boundary, keep the decision rights.

Muscle one · Process

Fall for the work, not the scoreboard

Alysa Liu quit skating at sixteen with nothing left to want, then came back for no reason except that she missed the ice — and skated to a world title with the pressure gone. The result arrived when it stopped being the point. Train the same move: hold outcomes with open hands; put your grip on today’s rep.

Something you’re gripping Rewrite it as a process you’d enjoy Shape: “I can’t control ___. Today’s rep is ___ — and while I do it I get to enjoy ___.”
Muscle two · Boundary

Love them, and keep the last word

You can love your family with your whole heart and still keep the last word on your own life. In homes where love and control grew tangled, guilt arrives right on schedule — treat it as a toll on the road out, not a verdict on the route. Pay it, keep driving.

Kind-but-firm sentences, for rehearsing out loud

“I hear you, and I love you. I’ve decided to do this — and I’ll own how it turns out.”

“I’m not asking permission. I’m telling you because I respect you.”

“I’ll think about what you said. The decision stays with me.”

“I know you want good things for me. So do I. Let me show you, my way.”

“No.” — said gently, it is still a full sentence.

Muscle three · Money

Money is stored choice — keep the decision rights

Michael Jackson out-earned nearly everyone alive and still spent his last years leveraged, surrounded by people paid to agree, signed into things he hadn’t fully read. The balance was never the problem; the decision rights were. Wealth frees you only if the choices stay yours.

Know where every rupee sits. If you can’t sketch your money on a napkin, someone else effectively holds it.
Never sign what you don’t understand. “My people looked at it” is how empires leak.
Grow a Freedom Fund. Every month of runway is one more month you can say no — to a boss, a deal, anyone.
Watch lifestyle inflation. Every new fixed cost is a new boss you just hired.
