The method

Why staged practice beats an unstructured sit.

Chanting for thirty minutes and attending to the breath's pleasantness is a fine practice — but it asks you to do everything at once, with no feedback. Stillpoint splits the path to absorption (jhāna) into four stages, gives each one exactly one instruction, and then fades its own supports so the state can take over.

1 · Settle

Paced breathing at roughly five to six breaths per minute, exhale longer than inhale. Breathing near this "resonance frequency" measurably raises vagal tone and heart-rate variability — it drops the body into parasympathetic calm in minutes, giving you a physiological head start that unpaced chanting doesn't.

2 · Anchor

The pacer fades; the breath returns to its own rhythm. You rest attention where the breath is most vivid and simply begin again each time the mind wanders. Occasional soft bells act as attention probes — research on mind-wandering shows people drift for minutes without noticing; an external check shortens that lag while you're still training the skill. The bells space out and stop as the sit deepens.

3 · Glow

This is the pivot most self-taught meditators miss, and the heart of Leigh Brasington's jhāna method: once attention is steady, deliberately shift it from the breath to the most pleasant sensation available — warmth, tingling, the smile itself — and give that pleasantness your full attention. Piti (energetic joy) feeds on attention. A slight smile genuinely helps.

4 · Absorb

Everything the app was doing stops. The drone recedes, the screen goes dark, and there are no more prompts — because at this point any external cue is an anchor pulling you back out. You let the pleasantness grow until it absorbs attention on its own. That letting-go, not effort, is what tips access concentration into jhāna.

After: rate the factors

You rate the four classical jhāna factors — stability of attention, piti, sukha (contented happiness), and effortlessness — plus how deep the sit went. This turns each session into deliberate practice with feedback instead of an unmeasured half hour. Watching the piti line rise over weeks tells you the method is working before the states themselves become obvious.

Honest notes

  • The optional theta binaural layer is marked experimental because the evidence for brainwave entrainment is genuinely mixed. The drone's value is steadiness, not magic frequencies.
  • No app produces jhāna. Consistency, retreat time, and unbroken attention do. This one just removes the common failure points: starting tense, wandering unnoticed, never pivoting to pleasantness, and getting no feedback.

Drawn from: Leigh Brasington, Right Concentration (the pleasantness-pivot protocol) · Lehrer & Gevirtz on resonance-frequency breathing and HRV · Culadasa, The Mind Illuminated (wandering detection, fading supports) · Sacchet et al.'s neuroimaging of advanced jhāna practitioners, which frames jhāna as trainable attention-plus-positive-affect rather than mystery.
