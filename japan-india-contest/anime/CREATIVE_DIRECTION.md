# *Two Lights, One Sky* (ふたつの光、ひとつの空)
## Contest teaser anime — creative direction

**Format:** ~30–40 s, 16:9, no dialogue, one line of narration at the end.
**Function:** announce the Kizuna–Bandhan contest and teach its premise — *perspective
exchange* — purely through film grammar, so it works untranslated in both countries.

---

## 1. Premise

Two girls who have never met. **Hikari** (光, "light"), 16, in a mid-sized Japanese town on
Tanabata evening. **Diya** (दीया, "lamp"), 16, in Jaipur on Diwali night. Each performs her
own festival's ritual of light and hope — and through a single match cut, the film asserts
they are doing the *same thing*. Their two skies join into one starfield. Title. Call to
entry. That's the whole film: the contest's thesis with no words needed.

The names are the theme: both mean light.

## 2. Why these two festivals

- **Tanabata** (July 7): wishes written on *tanzaku* paper strips, hung on bamboo under the
  Milky Way — a festival literally about two separated people (Orihime and Hikoboshi)
  allowed to meet across the sky once a year. It is the perfect built-in metaphor for a
  two-country contest, and it is the contest's launch date.
- **Diwali** (Oct/Nov): rows of *diyas* (oil lamps) lit on the darkest new-moon night —
  light as knowledge over ignorance, and homes opened to others. It is the contest's
  mid-campaign beat.
- Both send light upward against the dark; both are about hope addressed to someone not
  present. Different ritual, same gesture — which is the definition of cross-cultural
  understanding this contest wants to reward.

## 3. Direction — what we take from the masters

This teaser is deliberately built the way we ask entrants to work: study the greats of the
partner medium, and borrow *thinking*, not surfaces. One principle per shot.

### 3.1 Hayao Miyazaki — *ma* (間), and wind as emotion
Miyazaki famously described *ma* by clapping: "the time in between my clapping is *ma*. If
you just have non-stop action with no breathing space at all, it's just busyness." Our film
opens with that breathing space: Shot 1 is not an event, it is a held moment — dusk air, a
paper strip trembling. Miyazaki also lets wind do the acting; here the wind that lifts the
tanzaku is the first "character" on screen, and it is the thing that will carry us to India.

### 3.2 Satoshi Kon — the match cut as an argument
Kon's edits (in *Millennium Actress* and *Paprika*) cut across time and space on matched
shape and motion, so that two different worlds are experienced as one continuous thought.
Our hinge is Shot 2→3: the white flutter of the tanzaku **becomes** the flicker of a diya
flame — same screen position, same rhythm of movement, same warm key light. The edit *is*
the message: her wish and her lamp are one gesture. Nothing in the film works if this cut
doesn't.

### 3.3 Naoko Yamada — hands before faces
Yamada (*A Silent Voice*, *Liz and the Blue Bird*) locates feeling in the body's periphery:
hands, fingertips, feet, hair moved by breath — often framing *below* the face, because
gesture is less guarded than expression. Our Shot 3 stays on hands: Diya's fingers shielding
a flame, placing the lamp into a row on the rooftop parapet, a smear of rangoli powder on
her knuckle. Intimacy without a single close-up of eyes; the audience leans in.

### 3.4 Makoto Shinkai — the sky as the space between people
Shinkai's films (*Your Name.*, *5 Centimeters per Second*) use vast luminous skies and
light scatter to make distance itself emotional — two people connected precisely by what
separates them. Shot 4 is our Shinkai shot: camera tilts up from each girl and the two skies
— Tanabata's Milky Way, Diwali's fireworks and lamp-glow — merge into a single continuous
starfield. The film's title appears inside that shared sky.

### 3.5 Isao Takahata — the discipline of the ordinary
Takahata (*Only Yesterday*, *Grave of the Fireflies*) insisted that emotional truth lives in
accurately observed everyday detail, not in spectacle. This governs our art direction
everywhere: the tanzaku is slightly creased; the bamboo is a municipal shopping-street
decoration, not a fantasy grove; Diya's rooftop has a water tank, drying laundry, a distant
temple silhouette — a *real* Jaipur roof, not a postcard. Accuracy is respect, and accuracy
is also the contest's first judging criterion.

### 3.6 Production restraint (the whole tradition)
Classic TV anime holds on stillness and lets one element move (cloth, flame, hair). Every
shot here has exactly **one primary motion** — strip, flame, hands, sky — which keeps
AI-generated motion coherent and, more importantly, keeps the film calm enough to feel like
a wish rather than an advert.

## 4. Art direction

- **Palette:** Japan scenes — indigo dusk (藍色), paper-white, bamboo green, one warm
  lantern accent. India scenes — deep new-moon blue, marigold orange, terracotta, flame
  gold. The two palettes share the same warm accent (lantern ↔ diya) so the match cut lands.
- **Light:** golden-hour rim light in Japan; low warm key from below (lamp-lit faces) in
  India; final shot cools into a shared blue-violet night sky.
- **Design:** contemporary 2D anime look, painterly backgrounds, clean character lines.
  Hikari: school summer uniform or simple yukata, short dark hair. Diya: teal-and-gold
  salwar kameez, braid, glass bangles. Both drawn with the same line weight and eye style —
  one visual language for both countries, by design.
- **Cultural details to get right (Takahata rule):** tanzaku hung with string loops, wishes
  in handwritten Japanese; bamboo decoration scale of a shotengai; diyas are clay with
  visible wicks in oil, arranged along parapet edges; rangoli at the rooftop door; fireworks
  distant, not dominating; no religious iconography foregrounded — festivals shown as
  lived, domestic practice.

## 5. Sound

No dialogue. Ambient: evening cicadas + wind chime (Japan), distant firecrackers + night
breeze (India), converging into quiet under the title. One narrated line over the final
card, recorded in three languages for the three campaign regions:

> EN: "Two lights. One sky. Show us the world through each other's eyes."
> JA: 「ふたつの光、ひとつの空。互いの目で、世界を描こう。」
> HI: "दो रोशनी, एक आसमान। एक-दूसरे की नज़र से दुनिया दिखाओ।"

## 6. Production note (AI-assisted, disclosed)

The teaser itself follows the contest's AI policy: generated with image + video models from
a human-authored storyboard, with full disclosure of tools and workflow (see
`STORYBOARD.md`). Keyframes are generated first for character/palette control, then
animated image-to-video shot by shot, then cut together. The storyboard file is the
authoritative shot list.
