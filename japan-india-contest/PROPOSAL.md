# Kizuna–Bandhan (絆・बंधन)
## The Japan–India Cross-Cultural Understanding Contest — Proposal

> *Kizuna* (絆, Japanese) and *Bandhan* (बंधन, Hindi) both mean **"bond."*
> Two words, two scripts, one idea — the premise of this contest in a single title.

**Status:** Draft proposal v1.0 · July 2026
**Theme for the inaugural edition:** **"Two Lights, One Sky"** (ふたつの光、ひとつの空 / दो रोशनी, एक आसमान)

---

## 1. Executive summary

Kizuna–Bandhan is an annual, youth-focused creative contest that invites participants from
Japan and India to interpret each other's cultures — not their own — through short film and
anime, manga/comics, writing, visual art, food, and technology. The core mechanic is
**perspective exchange**: Japanese entrants create works about India, Indian entrants create
works about Japan, and a special "Tandem" track pairs one creator from each country on a
single joint entry.

The inaugural theme, *Two Lights, One Sky*, links two festivals of light and wishes —
**Tanabata** (paper wishes hung under the July stars) and **Diwali** (oil lamps lit under the
autumn new moon) — as a shared metaphor: different rituals, same human impulse to send hope
upward. A commissioned 30–40 second anime teaser (see `anime/`) carries this theme as the
contest's flagship promotional piece.

Target outcomes for year one: 5,000+ entries, 50 finalists, two reciprocal exchange trips,
and a travelling exhibition of winning works in Tokyo and New Delhi.

## 2. Background and rationale

- Japan and India marked **70+ years of diplomatic relations** (established 1952) and have
  deepened ties through the Special Strategic and Global Partnership, growing student
  mobility, and expanding business and technology corridors.
- Cultural flow is strong but **asymmetric and shallow**: anime, manga, and Japanese cuisine
  have enormous reach among Indian youth; Indian cinema, yoga, and cuisine are visible in
  Japan — yet mutual understanding often stops at surface aesthetics.
- Both countries share deep, under-explored connections: Buddhism's journey from India to
  Japan; the story of Bose and Japan; the sanskrit roots of characters seen in Japanese
  temples (Siddham script); shared festival grammars of light, harvest, and renewal.
- **Youth demographics are complementary**: India has one of the world's youngest populations;
  Japan seeks global engagement for its young creators. A creative contest is the
  lowest-friction, highest-emotion bridge between them.

## 3. Vision and objectives

**Vision.** A generation in both countries that sees the other not as an aesthetic or a
market, but as a neighbour whose festivals, foods, scripts, and stories they can read with
affection and accuracy.

**Objectives.**
1. Produce and celebrate creative work that demonstrates *accurate, respectful, curious*
   engagement with the partner culture.
2. Create direct person-to-person bonds (tandem entries, exchange trips, mentor circles).
3. Build a reusable, open archive of cross-cultural creative work for schools in both countries.
4. Establish an annual fixture that institutions (embassies, foundations, universities,
   studios) can co-own and grow.

## 4. Contest structure

### 4.1 Tracks

| # | Track | Entry format | Notes |
|---|-------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | **Anime & Short Film** | ≤ 5 min video | Flagship track; any technique (2D, 3D, live action, AI-assisted with disclosure) |
| 2 | **Manga & Comics** | 4–16 pages | Manga, webtoon, or Indian comics idiom welcome |
| 3 | **Essay & Short Story** | ≤ 2,000 words | Written in Japanese, Hindi, English, or any Indian scheduled language with English translation |
| 4 | **Visual Art & Design** | Single work or series ≤ 5 pieces | Illustration, poster, textile/pattern design, calligraphy across scripts |
| 5 | **Food Story** | Recipe + photo/video essay | A dish that carries both cultures (e.g., curry's Japanese journey, matcha in Indian sweets) |
| 6 | **Tech for Understanding** | Working prototype + demo video | Apps, games, or tools that teach language, festivals, or etiquette of the partner culture |

### 4.2 The perspective-exchange rule

- Entrants based in **Japan** create work engaging with **Indian** culture.
- Entrants based in **India** create work engaging with **Japanese** culture.
- **Tandem track (all formats):** one Japan-based + one India-based creator co-author a
  single entry. Tandem entries receive a dedicated jury prize, because the collaboration
  *is* the point of the contest.

### 4.3 Age divisions

- **Junior:** 13–17 (school outreach programme)
- **Open:** 18–30 (primary target)
- **Community:** 31+ (non-prize honourable mentions, keeps the door open to everyone)

### 4.4 Annual theme

Each edition sets one theme that works in both cultures. Year one: **"Two Lights, One Sky"**
(Tanabata × Diwali — wishes and light). Candidate future themes: *Monsoon and Tsuyu* (rain),
*Bento and Dabba* (the carried meal), *Kata and Riyaz* (practice and mastery), *Ma and
Shunya* (emptiness and space).

## 6. Judging

### 6.1 Criteria (100 points)

| Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|-----------|--------|------------------|
| Cultural insight & accuracy | 30 | Depth beyond stereotype; verifiable detail; evidence of research or lived exchange |
| Craft & execution | 25 | Quality within the chosen medium |
| Emotional resonance | 20 | Does it move a viewer from *either* country? |
| Originality of the bridge | 15 | Freshness of the connection drawn between the two cultures |
| Spirit of exchange | 10 | Generosity, curiosity, and respect visible in the work |

### 6.2 Jury

- Two **national juries** (working in local languages) shortlist per track.
- A **joint grand jury** of 8–10: filmmakers/animators, mangaka, authors, chefs, scholars of
  Japanese studies (India) and South Asian studies (Japan), plus one youth juror per country
  selected from the previous year's finalists (from year two onward).
- Cultural-sensitivity review is part of shortlisting, not an afterthought: reviewers from
  the *depicted* culture confirm each finalist work before publication.

### 6.3 AI-assisted work policy

AI-assisted entries are permitted in all tracks **with full disclosure** of tools and
workflow. Judging rewards authorship — concept, cultural research, direction, and editing —
and a separate "process appendix" lets juries weigh human contribution fairly. Undisclosed
AI use is grounds for disqualification.

## 8. Organisation and partners

**Proposed structure:** a small joint secretariat (2 staff per country) hosted by an existing
cultural institution, with a steering committee of the principal partners.

| Role | Candidate partners |
|------|--------------------|
| Principal cultural partners | The Japan Foundation (New Delhi), Indian Council for Cultural Relations (Tokyo) |
| Government patronage | Embassy of Japan in India; Embassy of India in Japan; Japan–India Association |
| Education outreach | CBSE/KVS networks & Japanese-language departments (JNU, DU, Visva-Bharati); Japanese universities with South Asian studies (TUFS, Kyoto U) |
| Industry | Anime studios & production committees (track 1 mentoring), publishers (track 2), food media (track 5), tech companies with Japan–India footprints (track 6 + funding) |
| Media | NHK World / Doordarshan for finale broadcast; youth platforms for campaign reach |

## 10. Promotion — and the anime teaser

The campaign leads with a **30–40 second original anime teaser**, *Two Lights, One Sky*,
released on Tanabata. It tells the entire contest premise without dialogue: a girl in Japan
hangs a wish; a girl in India lights a lamp; a match cut joins their two skies into one.
Creative direction, storyboard, and production notes are in [`anime/`](anime/) — the piece
is itself built the way we hope entrants will work: by studying the masters of the medium
and the details of the partner culture.

Secondary assets: poster series (track key art), 15s vertical cutdowns per festival moment,
school toolkit, and a tandem-matchmaking mini-site.

## 11. Values, safety, and IP

- **Respect over exotics:** guidance for entrants on stereotype pitfalls, with examples;
  sensitivity reviewers from the depicted culture at shortlist stage.
- **Language access:** all rules and mentor sessions in Japanese, Hindi, and English.
- **IP:** creators retain copyright; the contest receives a non-exclusive licence for
  exhibition, archive, and promotion with attribution.
- **Safeguarding:** junior-division communication runs through schools/guardians; tandem
  matchmaking for minors is school-to-school only.

## 12. Success metrics (year one)

- ≥ 5,000 valid entries with a 45/55–55/45 country balance
- ≥ 300 tandem entries (the bellwether metric — bonds formed, not just works submitted)
- ≥ 500 schools reached; teaser reaching 5M+ organic views across both markets
- Post-finale survey: ≥ 70% of finalists report an ongoing collaboration or friendship
  across the two countries at the 6-month mark
