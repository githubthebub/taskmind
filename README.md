# taskmind — Every Second

A practice room for your brain. Open `index.html` and step in.

Built around one idea, borrowed from an athlete who stopped caring about
medals and started loving the work: **enjoy the process, and the outcome
loses its weight.**

## How it works

1. **Step in.** Name the thing you're practicing — the verb, not the prize.
   If what you type sounds like an outcome ("win", "finish", "perfect"),
   the room gently asks what the doing underneath it is. You can keep it
   anyway.
2. **Be there.** The clock counts *up* — there is no target to miss, only
   seconds gained. A rotating line of fuel keeps the frame. When asked how
   it feels, you can **ease off**, **hold**, or **push** — backing off is
   treated as a skill, not a failure.
3. **Step back.** Two questions, neither about how it went: *what did you
   feel?* (anger counts exactly as much as joy) and *what did you notice?*
   Keep it, or let it go.

## What it refuses to do

- No streaks, so no streak can be broken.
- No totals, no charts, no medals, no scores.
- No pause-button guilt, no red "you failed" states.
- Rough entries stay in the record — they're load-bearing.

## Tech

One self-contained HTML file. No build, no dependencies, no network —
fonts are inlined, sessions live in your browser's localStorage and never
leave your machine. A session in progress survives a closed tab: the
clock never loses your time.
